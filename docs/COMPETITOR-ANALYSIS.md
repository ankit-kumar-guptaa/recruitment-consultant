## The four named competitors are not one competitor set

Rajiv sir named four sites, but they compete with us in three different ways,
and only one of them is actually beatable this year.

### Group A — Global staffing brands: Randstad India, ManpowerGroup India

| | |
| --- | --- |
| What they are | Subsidiaries of global staffing groups (Netherlands and USA), multi-billion-dollar parents |
| Estimated referring domains | Thousands, including press, academic and government citations |
| Why they rank | Domain authority inherited from a global brand, plus two decades of indexed history and large job-board sections that attract links and brand searches |
| Where they are weak | Generic service copy written to a global template. Their "staffing services India" page says little an Indian hiring manager could not have guessed. Almost no genuinely local or sector-specific depth. |
| **Can we beat them?** | **On head terms: no, not in year one.** On specific employer-intent long tail: yes, routinely — they have not written those pages at all. |

### Group B — Established Indian consultancy: ABC Consultants

| | |
| --- | --- |
| What they are | One of India's oldest recruitment firms (founded 1969), strong executive search reputation |
| Estimated referring domains | Hundreds to low thousands, heavily India-weighted — which makes them *more* relevant to Indian SERPs than a global parent's link profile |
| Why they rank | Age, genuine domain expertise, real case studies and named consultants, consistent PR presence in Indian business press |
| Where they are weak | Positioned at the senior/executive end. Thinner on volume hiring, contract staffing, plant-level and frontline roles — a large part of what we actually do. |
| **Can we beat them?** | Not on brand or executive search. **Yes on contract staffing, bulk hiring, plant and frontline roles**, where their content is thin and ours is specific. |

### Group C — Niche specialist: JAC Recruitment India

| | |
| --- | --- |
| What they are | Japanese-origin recruitment firm, specialising in Japanese and multinational companies operating in India |
| Why they rank | Narrow, well-defined niche with low competition and high intent — exactly the strategy we are copying, just in a different niche |
| Where they are weak | Deliberately narrow. They are not trying to rank for general Indian staffing terms. |
| **Can we beat them?** | Irrelevant question — we barely overlap. **But they are the best template to study.** JAC proves that a mid-sized firm beats global brands by owning a niche completely instead of competing broadly. |

## The competitors Rajiv sir did not name — and they matter more

On most employer-intent SERPs in India, the real competition for a click is not
the four sites above:

1. **Naukri.com, LinkedIn, Indeed, Shine.** They occupy a large share of
   recruitment SERPs and are unbeatable on volume. The upside: they rank for
   *jobseeker* intent far more than employer intent, which is precisely why the
   employer-intent strategy avoids colliding with them.
2. **Hundreds of small independent agencies.** 10–40 page sites, thin content,
   almost no technical SEO. **These are the actual competition for tier 1 and
   tier 2 keywords, and we already out-build them** — our sector pages run
   1,700–1,800 words of specific content against their 300 words of
   boilerplate.
3. **IndiaMART and JustDial listing pages.** They rank for
   "recruitment agency in <city>" style queries, and they are listings, not
   agencies. Getting listed there is both a referring domain and a direct lead
   source. In P1-5 of the pending list for that reason.

## Content gap: what we have that they do not

This is the part worth protecting, because it is the whole basis of the plan.

| Our asset | Why it wins |
| --- | --- |
| 16 role pages with salary bands, time-to-hire and interview structure | Almost nobody in Indian recruitment publishes this. It is exactly what a hiring manager searches for, and exactly what AI assistants quote. |
| 12 sector pages with real hiring constraints — audit exposure, notice-period behaviour, where the talent sits | Globals write one generic page. We wrote twelve specific ones. |
| 17 city pages with actual local hubs and market behaviour | Most agency "city" pages are the same paragraph with the city swapped. Ours are not, which is also why they will survive Google's scaled-content policy. |
| Honesty as positioning — we publish what will make a role hard to fill | Unusual in this market, and it converts: it reads like a consultant, not a vendor. |
| Technical foundation: SEO 100, accessibility 100, clean schema, zero broken links | Independent-agency sites are usually poor here. A cheap, real edge. |

## What to do about each group

- **Group A (globals):** do not compete on head terms this year. Take the long
  tail they ignore. Revisit in year two once referring domains pass ~60.
- **Group B (ABC):** compete where they are thin — contract staffing, bulk
  hiring, plant and frontline. Avoid executive search positioning until we have
  case studies to back it.
- **Group C (JAC):** copy the *method*, not the niche. Own a category
  completely. Our equivalent is becoming the obvious answer for plant,
  frontline and volume hiring in the specific industrial belts we name.
- **Job boards:** stay out of their way. Employer intent only.
- **Small agencies:** this is the fight we are in and already winning on
  content. What decides it is the three critical gaps above — case studies,
  named people, Business Profiles. Content alone will not close it.

## Re-run this properly when you can

What would make this document trustworthy, cheapest first:

2. **Google Search Console** — free, and once P0-1 is done it shows which
